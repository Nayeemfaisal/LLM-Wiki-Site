Literature note · 13 October 2026

Two papers sharpen the next experiment: extract pulse features carefully, then test whether they add value.

The papers do not validate our pipeline by themselves. They clarify two useful comparison tracks: pulse-relaxation diagnostics and time-series augmentation for state estimation.

Research decision

Keep diagnostics and augmentation separate

One paper concerns extracting EIS-like diagnostics from pulse relaxation. The other concerns generating or augmenting time-series data for SoC estimation. Both are relevant, but neither proves that our matched TS–EIS–DRT pipeline works.

Paper 1 · preprint

Lie Generator Networks: pulse relaxation as an EIS-grade diagnostic

A1

What it proposes

The authors propose using post-pulse voltage relaxation to estimate time constants that they describe as carrying EIS-grade diagnostic information.

A2

Why it matters here

It is a direct comparator for our time-domain DRT direction: both start with a current disturbance and a voltage-relaxation response.

A3

How we will use it

Compare only on matched held-out cells/states. Report reconstruction error, rank correlation, residuals, stability, and uncertainty rather than relying on a visual curve match.

Limit: this is an arXiv preprint. Its reported performance is the authors' claim and requires independent reproduction before it can be treated as established evidence.

Paper 2 · Journal of Power Sources, 2023

Time-series Wasserstein GAN for state-of-charge estimation

  • Published topic: a Wasserstein GAN approach for time-series data in lithium-ion battery SoC estimation.
  • Relevance: it motivates a later augmentation baseline when real TS coverage is sparse or unbalanced.
  • Boundary: generated TS data must never be presented as measured EIS or measured DRT evidence.
  • Use in this project: test augmentation only after a real, held-out matched TS–EIS subset exists; compare it against a no-augmentation baseline.

Next experiment

Concrete addition to the research plan

  1. Choose a small matched TS–EIS subset with cell ID, temperature, and state metadata.
  2. Use real pulse/rest records to derive time-domain diagnostic features.
  3. Generate the EIS-derived DRT reference only after quality checks.
  4. Evaluate a plain baseline, a time-domain feature method, and any augmentation method separately.
  5. Publish metrics and limitations for every held-out test.
